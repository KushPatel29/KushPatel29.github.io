FLAGSHIP · DATA PRODUCTS, BI & METRIC GOVERNANCE

Retail Analytics Platform

Nine accountable data products now connect source evidence to operating decisions—and the release fails closed when its proof is stale or incomplete.

Read the case study

Scope. A retail chain's merchandising and replenishment platform, not a dashboard: incremental ETL into a day-partitioned parquet lake, a DuckDB query layer, row-level security down to one manager's own store book, and sixteen pages published as a prerendered site with no runtime data requests. A new Data Product Control Room gives executives one place to verify release evidence before trusting the dashboards.

Method. A machine-readable registry binds nine dbt-backed products to an accountable owner, steward, decision, grain, consumers, 27 quality gates, freshness SLA and recovery target. The control room validates each mart against schema and SQL, maps all 62 definitions — 47 implemented and 15 withheld, and reads the active dataset manifest for observed refresh, volume, coverage, source and version. Complete contracts plus current evidence produce GO; stale evidence produces REVIEW; missing or invalid proof produces NO-GO.

Findings. Revenue matched to the cent across four pages while Suppliers reported 22.3% margin against 22.96%, and Products $32.5M against $51.0M. Neither was the margin formula. Suppliers treated a recorded zero as a missing cost and charged $333,573 for 3,056 lines that never shipped; Products summed the top 200 SKUs of 880. Separately, eleven customer-economics cells had rendered em-dashes since the day they shipped — computed, returned by the API, and dropped before the template.

Actions. Both cost expressions corrected and the eleven cells wired to the payload that already held them. The KPI diff is now a test that fails the build on either defect. The control room adds a deliberate 90-second interview path: verify the run, trace accountability, then challenge the formula and grain. Forecast accuracy leads with WAPE 11.6% and a 40% hit rate from a rolling-origin backtest, with MAPE demoted to a labelled diagnostic.

Limitations. ROMI is withheld: spend and wins exist, exposure and a counterfactual do not, and the standard formula would credit marketing with every dollar the company moved. Inventory turnover appears twice on different bases, each stated on screen rather than quietly reconciled. Excess and dead stock need a SKU×date snapshot the order-line grain cannot give, so they are absent instead of wrong.

DATA PLATFORM · FABRIC PATTERNS

Supply Chain Control Tower

Bad data gets caught at the boundary — and the network's real problem turns out to be where the stock is, not how much.

Approach and evidence

Bad data doesn't announce itself; it quietly changes a number an executive already screenshotted. So this pipeline defends in three layers — schema contracts stop drift before Bronze, poisoned rows quarantine with replay, a quality gate guards Gold — and CI proves each layer by injecting the exact failure it exists to catch. On top of it sits the analysis a multinational actually runs: an approved vendor list across eleven countries (25 SKUs have no plan B; one origin carries 29.7% of COGS), OTIF decomposed into the two failures it hides (short-shipping, not lateness, is the bigger driver), and a planner's stock position where the shortfall grew 15.2% while total stock barely moved — the network rotating inventory into the lanes that need it least. Then the inbound half: 11,604 purchase-order lines scored on delivery, quality, cost and responsiveness against published targets rather than against each other, where 18 of 24 suppliers sit in a different band than their contract calls them — the contractual tier is never an input to the score, so the gap is a measurement, not a restatement. And the service policy nobody had ever priced: the textbook ABC ladder, applied at constant spend, loses 1.72 points of unit fill, because it protects revenue and a fill rate on units is not revenue.

FORECASTING · GIS · PUBLIC DATA

108 tests ✓

Canada Wildfire Risk

Rank Canada’s 771 fire cells every morning — and say plainly where fire weather alone does just as well.

Approach and evidence

A first-prize 2024 hackathon project, rebuilt from a yearly area-burned regression into a daily seven-day forecast on real public records: 164,707 fires from the National Fire Database, 3,198 CWFIS weather stations and 4.1 million cell-days. The Canadian Fire Weather Index System is implemented from Van Wagner and replayed against CWFIS’s published codes before anything relies on it. Two XGBoost models — a classifier and a Poisson count — are averaged and isotonically calibrated, trained on 2000–2016, tuned on 2017–2019 and scored once on 2020–2024, beside two baselines: each cell’s normal rate for the month and an FWI regression. Re-ranked each morning, the riskiest tenth of cells held 36.7% of the 26,975 fires reported. The 2026 season is graded against satellite hotspots, where FWI alone nearly matches the model, and the README says so. GitHub Actions rebuilds the forecast twice a day from live CWFIS and Open-Meteo data, and an eight-page Power BI dashboard is generated from spec and reconciled to the same evidence files. The original random forest is rebuilt too: its R² falls from 0.351 on a random split to 0.201 out of time.

PRICING & MARGIN · DECISION SUPPORT

Pricing & Costing Analytics

The list price is not the price. 18.2% of it never arrives — and this says which points to go and get.

Approach and evidence

A pricing analyst's whole job, from a vendor invoice to a decision somebody has to defend on Monday. Margin is expressed on pocket price — after every discount, rebate, payment term, freight allowance and return — because a margin quoted on list is the number that lets a deal look healthy while losing money. The output is not an elasticity, it is a sentence: six ordered actions across 240 products, worth $2.65M of annual gross margin, each carrying the reason it was chosen and a confidence about the evidence rather than the size of the prize.

The governed decision room makes the operating boundary explicit. It registers all 240 proposals, routes each material request to named authority, leaves 161 human decisions open, and reports a 6 pass / 2 review / 0 block release posture. It then follows 2,075 approved historical changes through fixed three-month pre/post windows; 1,132 stay on an explanation queue instead of becoming a benefit claim. The monitor is labelled observational, and the public app cannot approve or publish a customer price.

The part I would ask about first: the dataset is built so the analysis can be caught being wrong. Elasticity, cost pass-through and segment price sensitivity are seeded deliberately and taken back out through the real code — estimated category elasticities preserve the seeded ordering at a rank correlation of 0.95, and every pass-through estimate lands attenuated toward zero, which is what a regression on a noisy realisation of a decision does. Getting there took four attempts: price that moves only with time is collinear with the trend, and no elasticity is recoverable from it at all.

Underneath, the ERP layer arrives the way an ERP does — SAP-shaped billing items with missing standard costs, duplicated lines, UOM mismatches and orphan customers injected on purpose. All twelve quality rules find something, and the reconciliation walks the extract down to the staged total with every exclusion named and $0.00 unexplained. Six marts are computed a second time in DuckDB and held to the pandas ones at 1e-8, which is how the price-band median was caught being five percent apart under the same column name.

METHOD

My favourite results are the ones where I lost.

Anyone can show you the model that won. I'd rather show you the one I killed and the boring baseline that beat it, because choosing what not to ship is most of this job.

Read my modelling and validation approach

84.9% > 81.2%

The ranker lost

Two-stage retrieval with a gradient-boosted ranker, beaten by plain collaborative filtering on a warm-customer basket holdout; the ranker uses customer-disjoint label training. The fixed benchmark is checked in CI.

18.5% < 19.6%

The forecaster lost

A gradient-boosted multi-step forecaster lost to a moving average on WAPE. The moving average ships; the challenger's defeat is written into the README rather than quietly deleted.

p ≈ 0.23

The alarm I could not clear

A fairness screen fired on the attrition model. Fisher's exact came back at p ≈ 0.23, which is not evidence the disparity is noise — a non-significant result on a small group means the sample cannot settle the question, not that there is nothing there. So the ratio and its uncertainty stay published and the group stays on a monitor list, rather than the alarm being written off.

HOW A NUMBER GETS ONTO A DASHBOARD

STEP 01

Agree the grain

Before a single measure, the business and I settle what one row means. Most reporting disputes are definition disputes wearing a costume.

STEP 02

Contract the source

Schema contracts at the boundary, so upstream drift fails loudly at ingestion instead of silently in a Gold table.

STEP 03

Model it once

Star schema, one semantic layer, measures reviewed like code — so there's exactly one place a KPI can be wrong, and one place to fix it.

STEP 04

Test the claim

Every headline number gets a test: control totals, ceilings, negative cases. If a claim stops being true, CI goes red.

STEP 05

Ship it auditable

The number arrives with its lineage, its SQL and its access rules attached, so anyone can check my work without asking me.

Beat the baseline or don't ship

Every model is measured against the dumbest thing that could work. If the moving average wins, the moving average ships.

Governance before dashboards

RLS, masking and k-anonymity go in before the first visual, because retrofitting access control is how sensitive data leaks into a screenshot.

Prove the failure path

It isn't enough that the happy path works. I break the data on purpose in CI and prove the guard catches it.

Every repo has a short section on what I deliberately didn't build: no vector database where a SQL join was the right answer, no deep learning on 38 SKUs, no cloud architecture I couldn't defend in a budget meeting. Restraint is harder to demo than complexity, so I write it down.
