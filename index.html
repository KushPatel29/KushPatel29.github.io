FLAGSHIP · BI PLATFORM & METRIC GOVERNANCE

Retail Analytics Platform

Five pages reporting the same company disagreed about it. They reconcile now, and a test says so on every push.

Read the case study

Scope. A retail chain's merchandising and replenishment platform, not a dashboard: incremental ETL into a day-partitioned parquet lake, a DuckDB query layer, row-level security down to one manager's own store book, and sixteen pages published as a prerendered site with no runtime data requests.

Method. Every metric that appears on more than one page is defined once. The catalogue governs 62 definitions — 47 implemented with a formula, grain, source, owner and gross-or-net basis, and 15 withheld, each naming the source system it would need. Then the check that matters: query every page's bundle under one filter and diff every shared KPI.

Findings. Revenue matched to the cent across four pages while Suppliers reported 22.3% margin against 22.96%, and Products $32.5M against $51.0M. Neither was the margin formula. Suppliers treated a recorded zero as a missing cost and charged $333,573 for 3,056 lines that never shipped; Products summed the top 200 SKUs of 880. Separately, eleven customer-economics cells had rendered em-dashes since the day they shipped — computed, returned by the API, and dropped before the template.

Actions. Both cost expressions corrected and the eleven cells wired to the payload that already held them. The KPI diff is now a test that fails the build on either defect. Forecast accuracy leads with WAPE 11.6% and a 40% hit rate from a rolling-origin backtest, with MAPE demoted to a labelled diagnostic because small SKU-month denominators make it least reliable where it looks most alarming.

Limitations. ROMI is withheld: spend and wins exist, exposure and a counterfactual do not, and the standard formula would credit marketing with every dollar the company moved. Inventory turnover appears twice on different bases, each stated on screen rather than quietly reconciled. Excess and dead stock need a SKU×date snapshot the order-line grain cannot give, so they are absent instead of wrong.

PRICING & MARGIN · DECISION SUPPORT

Pricing & Costing Analytics

The list price is not the price. 18.2% of it never arrives — and this says which points to go and get.

Approach and evidence

A pricing analyst's whole job, from a vendor invoice to a decision somebody has to defend on Monday. Margin is expressed on pocket price — after every discount, rebate, payment term, freight allowance and return — because a margin quoted on list is the number that lets a deal look healthy while losing money. The output is not an elasticity, it is a sentence: six ordered actions across 240 products, worth $2.65M of annual gross margin, each carrying the reason it was chosen and a confidence about the evidence rather than the size of the prize.

The part I would ask about first: the dataset is built so the analysis can be caught being wrong. Elasticity, cost pass-through and segment price sensitivity are seeded deliberately and taken back out through the real code — estimated category elasticities preserve the seeded ordering at a rank correlation of 0.95, and every pass-through estimate lands attenuated toward zero, which is what a regression on a noisy realisation of a decision does. Getting there took four attempts: price that moves only with time is collinear with the trend, and no elasticity is recoverable from it at all.

Underneath, the ERP layer arrives the way an ERP does — SAP-shaped billing items with missing standard costs, duplicated lines, UOM mismatches and orphan customers injected on purpose. All twelve quality rules find something, and the reconciliation walks the extract down to the staged total with every exclusion named and $0.00 unexplained. Six marts are computed a second time in DuckDB and held to the pandas ones at 1e-8, which is how the price-band median was caught being five percent apart under the same column name.

DATA PLATFORM · FABRIC PATTERNS

Supply Chain Control Tower

Bad data gets caught at the boundary — and the network's real problem turns out to be where the stock is, not how much.

Approach and evidence

Bad data doesn't announce itself; it quietly changes a number an executive already screenshotted. So this pipeline defends in three layers — schema contracts stop drift before Bronze, poisoned rows quarantine with replay, a quality gate guards Gold — and CI proves each layer by injecting the exact failure it exists to catch. On top of it sits the analysis a multinational actually runs: an approved vendor list across nine countries (3 SKUs have no plan B; one origin carries 27% of COGS), OTIF decomposed into the two failures it hides (short-shipping, not lateness, is the bigger driver), and a planner's stock position where the shortfall grew 17% while total stock barely moved — the network rotating inventory into the lanes that need it least. Then the inbound half: 3,106 purchase-order lines scored on delivery, quality, cost and responsiveness against published targets rather than against each other, where 9 of 15 suppliers sit in a different band than their contract calls them — the contractual tier is never an input to the score, so the gap is a measurement, not a restatement. And the service policy nobody had ever priced: the textbook ABC ladder, applied at constant spend, loses 0.57 points of unit fill, because it protects revenue and a fill rate on units is not revenue.

CAPSTONE · GOVERNED AI

Ask Your Data

Ask 71 tables a question — typed or spoken — and audit the SQL behind every number.

Approach and evidence

One rule: no number without a query. The live demo has no API key and does not need one — a deterministic compiler binds your words to the warehouse's own columns, values and join graph, and its 58-question contract records 46 correct answers, 0 incorrect and 12 refusals. This measures the fixed contract, not accuracy on every possible question. Add a key and a language model writes the SQL instead, through the same read-only guard, structural verifier and access policy; its prose is then checked back against the returned rows, so a sentence cannot quote a number the query never produced. Six certified metrics carry an owner and a CI-asserted value. Speech runs inside the process on open models, so it listens and answers aloud with no account and no second service.

91 tests ✓

Marketing Attribution & Incrementality

I built a dataset where the right answer is known — then watched every attribution model fail to find it.

Approach and evidence

Attribution is the one analytics discipline where everybody argues and nobody can check, because the counterfactual isn't in the data. So this generates one: each user carries a fixed random draw, and a channel's true contribution is measured by re-running that same draw with its touches deleted. Six models compete. The exact Shapley value and a Markov chain both lose to a 40/20/40 heuristic — because every model that reads only a journey log sees how often a channel was present, never whether it caused anything. Last-touch hands direct a quarter of all conversions against a true share of 1.9%. A second act asks the same questions of a B2B SaaS motion — CRM pipeline, an ARR waterfall, NRR versus GRR, quota and capacity — and finds the segment with the fastest cycle and the best coverage is the one that loses money.

HR Attrition Analytics

A 4.5% gender pay gap that is mostly the exchange rate, and attrition that follows the manager rather than the market.

Approach and evidence

People data raises the stakes, so the guardrails came before the model: k-anonymity masking, a disparate-impact gate in CI (four-fifths rule plus Fisher's exact), survival analysis honest about censoring, and a flight-risk model that uses zero protected attributes. Across 2,800 people in eight countries the standard metrics stop working: pay is set to each local market, so most of a level's apparent gap is geography (Senior Manager reads 18.2% pooled, 3.4% inside a country); an attrition rate counts a managed-out low performer the same as a resigning star, and 56% of every exit happens in the first year. "Attrition clusters by manager" is held to a permutation test that passed with the effect switched off twice before it controlled for market and seniority.

METHOD

My favourite results are the ones where I lost.

Anyone can show you the model that won. I'd rather show you the one I killed and the boring baseline that beat it, because choosing what not to ship is most of this job.

Read my modelling and validation approach

84.9% > 81.2%

The ranker lost

Two-stage retrieval with a gradient-boosted ranker, beaten by plain collaborative filtering on a warm-customer basket holdout; the ranker uses customer-disjoint label training. The fixed benchmark is checked in CI.

18.5% < 19.6%

The forecaster lost

A gradient-boosted multi-step forecaster lost to a moving average on WAPE. The moving average ships; the challenger's defeat is written into the README rather than quietly deleted.

p ≈ 0.23

The alarm I could not clear

A fairness screen fired on the attrition model. Fisher's exact came back at p ≈ 0.23, which is not evidence the disparity is noise — a non-significant result on a small group means the sample cannot settle the question, not that there is nothing there. So the ratio and its uncertainty stay published and the group stays on a monitor list, rather than the alarm being written off.

HOW A NUMBER GETS ONTO A DASHBOARD

STEP 01

Agree the grain

Before a single measure, the business and I settle what one row means. Most reporting disputes are definition disputes wearing a costume.

STEP 02

Contract the source

Schema contracts at the boundary, so upstream drift fails loudly at ingestion instead of silently in a Gold table.

STEP 03

Model it once

Star schema, one semantic layer, measures reviewed like code — so there's exactly one place a KPI can be wrong, and one place to fix it.

STEP 04

Test the claim

Every headline number gets a test: control totals, ceilings, negative cases. If a claim stops being true, CI goes red.

STEP 05

Ship it auditable

The number arrives with its lineage, its SQL and its access rules attached, so anyone can check my work without asking me.

Beat the baseline or don't ship

Every model is measured against the dumbest thing that could work. If the moving average wins, the moving average ships.

Governance before dashboards

RLS, masking and k-anonymity go in before the first visual, because retrofitting access control is how sensitive data leaks into a screenshot.

Prove the failure path

It isn't enough that the happy path works. I break the data on purpose in CI and prove the guard catches it.

Every repo has a short section on what I deliberately didn't build: no vector database where a SQL join was the right answer, no deep learning on 38 SKUs, no cloud architecture I couldn't defend in a budget meeting. Restraint is harder to demo than complexity, so I write it down.
